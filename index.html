Background

Objective: To evaluate how the participants will apply the gained knowledge of H2S safety in realistic scenarios before completing the training.

This is a short, performance-based module designed to be learner-centered and highly interactive. It incorporates learner engagement elements such as decision-making scenarios and simulation-styled assessment of learners' knowledge.

This was a very tech-driven design process: it involved using Veo 3.1 for AI video generation, Gemini for AI image generation, webapp design and debugging, and Github for web hosting--using completely new workflows.

H2S Briefing

1. Physical Properties & Hazards

  • Heavier than air: Gas settles and concentrates in low-lying areas (trenches, cellars).
  • Colorless & Flammable: You cannot see it, and it can easily ignite.
  • Olfactory Fatigue: At low concentrations, it smells like rotten eggs. At high concentrations (or over time), it deadens your sense of smell completely. You cannot trust your nose.
  • Exposure Limit: 10 ppm (parts per million) is the threshold limit.
Pro-Tip: The 3 U's Since H2S is heavier than air, it settles in low areas. Always evacuate:
Upwind: Move into the wind (so the gas blows away from you).
Up: Get out of natural low-lying areas or confined spaces (trenches, cellars, pits).
Upward: Climb to a higher structural elevation (like a platform, rig floor, or upper deck).

2. Emergency Escape Protocol

If the stationary alarm sounds, your personal monitor goes off, or you suspect H2S gas, your only priority is self-preservation. Do not attempt a rescue.

  1. Stop work and hold your breath immediately.
  2. Check the wind direction by locating the nearest windsock.
  3. Don your EEBA (Emergency Escape Breathing Apparatus).
  4. Evacuate crosswind or upwind. Never run downwind in the same direction the gas is blowing.
  5. Proceed to the Safe Briefing Area (SBA) and report to the Muster Captain for a headcount. Keep your mask on until the all-clear is given.
